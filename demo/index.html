Photosensitivity warning

The combed patterns fill the picture with fine, high-contrast stripes, and the strokes move them. Gelgit, Chevron, Nonpareil (the plugin's default, and this page's), Bouquet and French Curl comb the inks into thin regular bands across the whole frame. Broadcast guidance on patterns (ITU-R BT.1702, which Ofcom's follows) treats fine, high-contrast regular stripes over a large part of the screen as a risk in themselves, whether or not anything flashes.

It can also change large areas at once, at the rate of the triggers (the music's drum hits, the beat, or your presses): a new drop pushes the ink around it outward, Auto Paper lays a fresh sheet over a finished pattern, and Suminagashi drops ink and clear water in turn at the same places. Patterns and flashing light like these can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. If you, or anyone who can see your screen, may be affected, do not play it.

Measured on all 33 of Resolume's demo clips at 60 fps, with their music or a 128 or 200 BPM beat driving the triggers, at the defaults and at the fastest, largest settings (also as Stone and Suminagashi), it never went over three flashes a second; its own worst was two a second. Your own clips and settings can differ.

The page runs on the plugin's own defaults; none was changed for this. Pause, under the picture, stops it at once.
